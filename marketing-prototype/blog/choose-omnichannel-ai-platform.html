What Should I Look for in an Omnichannel AI Platform?

A practical checklist for evaluating omnichannel AI platforms, from integrations and handoffs to permissions, data, and costs.

BEditorial TeamEditorial teamOct 23, 2026Updated Oct 23, 20262 min read

Start with a real workflow

Bring a representative request that starts in one channel, needs information from a connected system, and may end with a human. Ask the vendor to show the entire path. Check which channels work now, which need custom integration, and whether the same case history follows the customer. Distinguish a drafted answer from an action completed in your system.

Inspect the controls

Ask how knowledge is updated, how the agent shows uncertainty, and how permissions are scoped for each tool. Review identity checks, approval steps, audit records, retention, deletion, access controls, and incident response. Test a mistaken answer, a failed integration, and a customer asking for a person. The recovery path matters as much as the happy path.

Understand the operating cost

Compare the full cost of channels, messages or usage, integrations, setup, supervision, and ongoing content maintenance. Ask what data you can export if you leave. Get reporting for resolution, repeat contacts, complaints, transfers, and action errors. Assign an internal owner who can use that reporting to improve the service after launch.

Questions worth asking in writing

Request a diagram of data flow and a list of subprocessors. Ask which actions require your approval, how you can restrict an integration, how logs are retained, and how a case is exported. Have the vendor state which channels and languages are live today and which are planned. Then put those answers into a small proof of concept. A contractual feature list is useful, but an observed workflow is stronger evidence of fit.

The practical answer

Choose the platform that can demonstrate your workflow, its limits, and the controls your team will operate every day.
