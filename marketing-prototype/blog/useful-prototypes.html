Prototype the useful path

How a working page prototype can reveal the order, language, and actions a digital experience needs.

BEditorial TeamEditorial teamOct 23, 2026Updated Oct 23, 20263 min read

A prototype is most useful when it answers a real question. Can someone understand the offer? Can they find the information they need? Is the next action clear? Those questions guide what to build first.

Start with the journey

Choose one audience and one task. Sketch the path from their first question to a useful outcome. Keep the first version small enough to change when the path feels wrong.

Use real words early

Placeholder copy hides unclear ideas. Write a practical heading, a brief explanation, and action labels that describe what will happen. The words will show where the page needs more evidence or a different order.

Check the page at different widths

A wide layout can make a weak hierarchy look organized. Review the page on compact and wide screens. Make sure the reading order, controls, and key message still work when space changes.

Use what the prototype reveals to revise the content and flow. The goal is a clearer decision about the experience, not a polished page for its own sake.
