Make complex pages clear

A practical approach to making a web page's purpose, information, and next step easier to understand.

BEditorial TeamEditorial teamOct 23, 2026Updated Oct 23, 20263 min read

People usually arrive with a question or a task. They may need to understand a service, compare options, or decide whether to get in touch. A useful page helps them do that without making them decode the business first.

Lead with the visitor's question

Write down the question the page needs to answer. Put the plain answer near the top, then add detail for people who need it. A short heading and lead should tell someone what the page offers and why it matters.

Give the information a useful order

Group related details under descriptive headings. Show the outcome before the process, and explain unfamiliar terms where they appear. Each section should move the visitor closer to a decision.

Make one next step clear

The primary action should follow naturally from the page. It might be to explore a service, read a guide, or contact a person. Name the action plainly and place it where the visitor has enough context to use it.

Before adding more content, read the page as someone seeing it for the first time. If the purpose and next step still need an explanation, revise the page's order and words.
